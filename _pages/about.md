I hold a PhD and an MSc in International Relations from IRI/PUC-Rio, as well as a BSc in International Relations from UniverCidade. I also earned a BSc in Applied Mathematics from PUC-Rio and an MBA in Foreign Trade and International Business Management from FGV/RJ.

My master's research examined just war as a constitutive institution of the modern international system, drawing on the thought of Francisco de Vitoria. In my doctoral work, I employed an evolutionary game-theoretic approach to investigate how property came to be institutionally constituted, reproduced, and stabilized as a primary institution of political modernity.

I am currently focusing on topics such as digital sovereignty, global digital governance, cognitive defense, democratic backsliding, and a Darwinian evolutionary approach to the study of primary international institutions and international order.

Theoretically, my work is situated within an evolutionary constructivist framework that foregrounds the constitutive role of speech acts, meaning-making practices, and intersubjective recognition in the production and transformation of institutional reality. From this evolutionary perspective, international order is understood as grounded in historically contingent social imaginaries and relatively coherent symbolic universes that condition the possibilities for institutional articulation, stabilization, and emancipatory transformation.

More broadly, my research engages with International Relations theory, political theory, and critical security studies.
